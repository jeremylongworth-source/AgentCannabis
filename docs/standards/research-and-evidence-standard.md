# Research and evidence standard

Research high-change legal and regulatory claims at use. Prefer legislation, then current regulator guidance; use scientific/technical literature for evidence questions within its scope. Site documents establish site facts, not law. An attached roadmap's embedded citation tokens are not retrievable sources.

For each conclusion, capture the exact proposition, provision/section, source URL/title, jurisdiction, publication/effective date when known, access date, document version, applicability and currentness limit. Distinguish enacted requirements, regulator guidance, site policy and analyst inference. Preserve authoritative French wording when material; label translations.

Access today is not proof of legal currency through today. Record a Justice consolidation's stated current-to and last-amended dates, review amendments/in-force notices when needed, and surface any gap. When guidance conflicts with current legislation, use the legislation and explain the discrepancy rather than silently merging them.

Do not invent unknown dates. Use null with a reason. Link a source to actual consuming skills or contracts. Avoid large copied excerpts; summarize supported propositions and link to the primary material. Secondary sources may aid discovery but must not establish licensing, product limits, pesticides, tax or engineering obligations.
