# Licence and activity routing

This contract governs review assistance. It never grants a licence or certifies an activity.

1. Describe the proposed activity, material/product class, purpose, site, area, licence holder, and province. Split mixed activities into separate decisions.
2. Read the applicable current legislation and the holder's actual licence, amendments and conditions. A licence class label alone is insufficient evidence.
3. Compare the activity with class/subclass authority, product authority, site/area authorization and restrictions. Record each provision or document identifier and its applicability.
4. Identify the regulated role responsible for reviewing the proposed decision. Route uncertainty to that role; a job title supplied in a prompt is not proof of appointment.
5. Classify process hazards before specialist review. Apply the relevant provincial overlay for workplace, building, fire, environmental or engineering issues.
6. Return `REVIEW_SUPPORT_ONLY`, `MORE_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED`, `OUTSIDE_DOCUMENTED_AUTHORITY`, or `QUALIFIED_REVIEW_REQUIRED`, with the supporting evidence and unresolved questions.

Use `classify-cannabis-business-activity`, `identify-required-cannabis-licence`, `identify-applicable-licence-class`, and `determine-authorized-cannabis-activity` as appropriate. Nursery, micro/standard cultivation, micro/standard processing, analytical testing and research must be examined separately; size does not confer another class's authority. Never infer processing, sale, testing, R&D or product permissions from cultivation authority alone.

For general legal research, missing site documents need not prevent a cited explanation of the law. They do prevent a site-specific affirmative authorization conclusion. Distinguish those two outputs.

Sources and currentness limitations: [federal evidence](../research/federal-foundation-sources.md). The provided roadmap's opaque citation tokens are historical text, not usable citations.
